# Fault Tolerance at the Cognitive Level

The integration of foundation models into robotics changes both the scope and the meaning of fault tolerance. Classical robotic reliability was built around explicitly modelled worlds, bounded disturbances, and well-defined operational envelopes. In that setting, failures could often be attributed to sensor noise, state-estimation drift, controller instability, or hardware degradation. Once large language models, vision-language models, and vision-language-action systems are introduced, robotic failure acquires a cognitive dimension. A system may now fail because it misinterprets intent, hallucinates an object or affordance, loses track of state across a long reasoning chain, or produces an action sequence that is linguistically plausible but physically incoherent. Reliability in such systems therefore depends not only on physical robustness but also on runtime cognitive vigilance. [A Survey of Robot Intelligence with Large Language Models]
