## Background

Day by day, robotics gradually becomes an integral part of our lives. Robotics has been integrated into various sectors, including logistics, manufacturing, and healthcare. Although robots were initially limited to factories and surrounded by barriers, human interaction with them has increased. In sectors such as healthcare, a rapid increase in robots designed to coexist with humans can be seen. New research areas, such as Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), and new concepts, like socially aware robotics, have gained prominence.

Due to the dynamic nature of humans and the static predetermined nature of robots, it becomes a challenging task for robots to dynamically adjust themselves to suit humans when coexisting in the same environment. However, as robots increasingly integrate into sectors such as healthcare, their dynamic nature becomes necessary. For example, in socially aware robotic navigation, failure to achieve dynamic behaviour can result in phenomena such as *Freezing Robot Problem* and *Reciprocal Problem* \cite{trautman_robot_2015}.

Various studies have focused on enhancing the planning and control system to improve its dynamic nature when coexisting with humans. In social aware navigation research, many studies have proposed learning-based systems \cite{wang_navistar_2023, wang_feedback-efficient_2022} or have proposed generative behaviour-based systems by linking foundational models with existing behaviour-based systems \cite{izzo_btgenbot_2024, cao_robot_nodate} to attempt to solve either *Freezing Robot Problem* or the *Reciprocal Problem* as a way of achieving dynamic behaviour. Both approaches have advantages and disadvantages, which will be discussed in later chapters. One significant commonality between these research studies is that they primarily focus on passively interacting with humans with little to no communication between entities.

This study also aims to enhance the dynamic nature of robots. Moving beyond mainstream research, it proposes an active interaction-based approach to achieving dynamic behaviour while coexisting in human environments. To achieve this goal, this study proposes a new behaviour-based planning system built from the ground up to support foundational model integration, facilitating dynamic replanning capabilities. Finally, this study presents a socially aware assistive robot system as a use case to demonstrate the functionality of the proposed behaviour-based planning system.
