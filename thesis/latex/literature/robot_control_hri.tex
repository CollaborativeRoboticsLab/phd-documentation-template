\section{Robotics Systems for Human Robot Interaction}
\label{sec::LiteratureReview::RobotControlHRI}

Robotic systems designed for human-robot interaction (HRI) must balance the need for autonomous operation with the ability to respond to human actions and social cues. Traditional robotic control architectures, such as behaviour-based systems, provide a structured approach to robot control but often lack the flexibility required for dynamic and socially aware interactions. On the other hand, learning-based systems, including those leveraging generative models like large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), offer adaptability but are heavily dependent on the quality and diversity of their training datasets.
